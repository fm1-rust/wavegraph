# wavegraph-hoover

A four-voice, allocation-free Alpha Juno-style instrument. Dominator pairs a chopped PWM saw with a full-level, 25% pulse sub two octaves down. Fast Hoover also layers a PWM pulse at the played pitch. The voices feed a bass-boost/high-pass stage, a resonant four-pole low-pass and a stereo ensemble. One envelope moves pitch, filter and amplitude together. T1, T2 and T3 reach independent levels L1, L2 and L3. L3 holds until key-up. T4 releases from the instantaneous level, even during the initial sweep.

The default Dominator patch has a 31 ms low wind-up followed by a 95 ms surge to about five semitones above the played note. Hold the key to hear it settle back onto pitch over 460 ms. Release it at any point to let the pitch, brightness and volume fall together over 250 ms. A sixteenth-note stab catches the crest at 120 BPM. The [4:57 Extended Mix](https://music.apple.com/us/song/dominator-extended-mix/1173863125) has a repeated opening sweep with the same overshoot and fall.

On the FM-1 choose HOOVER on any channel. Knobs 1–4 edit cutoff, pitch sweep, PWM depth and chorus rate. Turn chorus to zero for the dry sound. On ENV, turn or press SELECT to cycle through TIME, LVL, DCO and MOD.

In unison mode all four oscillators restart together. Each runs 6 dB below its polyphonic level to leave room for their combined attack.

Human Resource describe building the [Dominator sound](https://www.rige.net/news/221) on an Alpha Juno-2 with a pitch envelope, sub oscillator and enough chorus to suggest another oscillator. In his [account of recording Mentasm](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2014/05/key-tracks-mundo-muzique-on-mentasm/), Mundo Muzique describes a pitch-bent MIDI performance, sampling the complete riff into a Casio FZ-1, then moving its filter and LFO. Dominator's envelope is a WaveGraph performance setting inspired by those records. For a later use, Marc Acardipane plays the opening of Ace the Space's 1992 “9 Is a Classic” and identifies its Hoover in his [Red Bull Music Academy lecture, 15:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-INotoOMGqs&t=924s).

ALGORITHM recalls four programs. Dominator is the default. One turn clockwise selects the original Fast Hoover, whose shorter release suits tightly spaced notes. What The uses the [factory times and levels](https://llamamusic.com/mks50/Alpha_HS_APR_Patch_Sample_Printout.PDF), including levels 68/127/127, on WaveGraph's approximate time scale. Twin Peak is a softer polysynth patch whose shared contour opens, dips and swells again under a held chord.

Boochow's [Hoover oscillator walkthrough](https://blog.boochow.com/article/hoover-for-nts1.html) documents the pulse, chopped saw and quarter-duty sub. The GPL [JC-303](https://midilab.co/docs/dsp/jc303/) also offers an Alpha Juno oscillator. The WaveGraph signal path is an independent implementation.

The DCO page selects the pulse, saw and sub waveform and the sub level. The level page also exposes resonance on knob 4. MOD sets the four-position high-pass, filter envelope depth, PWM rate and noise level. At PWM rate zero the depth control sets a static width. These controls follow the waveform and HPF diagrams in the [Roland manual, pp. 20–22](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JUNO-1_OM.pdf). The scope emblem is a 16 × 32 reduction of the public-domain [upright vacuum cleaner by Anonymous](https://openclipart.org/detail/13321/vacuum-cleaner-upright).

The FM-1 sends the stereo ensemble to headphones and USB on a clean strip. Channels 3 and 4 start with empty insert slots. Channels 1 and 2 retain their 303 inserts when reassigned. A serial insert or active Beat Flanger, Slicer or Pattern Filter takes the mono fold. Faders and the stereo master still apply to both sides. The sampler clipboard records the mono fold.

## Modeling the chorus

Roland's block diagram and main-board schematic show independent MN3101 clock drivers and MN3009 bucket-brigade delays on the two outputs. Both receive the same filtered input. The second clock control is inverted, so one side sharpens as the other flattens. Each return has its own reconstruction filter and mixes with dry audio in the same polarity. The returning signals have nearly the same low-frequency mix resistance as the dry feed. The chorus switch gates the return while the direct path stays connected. [MKS-50 service notes, printed pp. 4 and 11](https://www.kiwitechnics.com/downloads/Kiwi-2/Roland%20MKS-50%20Service%20Manual.pdf)

Two open-source implementations illustrate different choices. June-21 models the Alpha Juno with two variable delays driven a quarter-cycle apart. Junologue targets the Juno-60 on embedded hardware and reads two opposing delays from a filtered delay line. WaveGraph uses the MKS-50 schematic for the relationship between the two clocks and published Alpha Juno hardware measurements for the rate control. Setting 54 is about 0.17 Hz, 92 is about 1.45 Hz, and 127 reaches about 10.1 Hz. WaveGraph reserves rate zero for bypass. [June-21 chorus](https://github.com/mikerodd/june-21/blob/b46c09a0548cdbe1d23089c9406f3c67d4a83fb0/src/cabbage-module/june-21.csd#L975-L991) · [Junologue DSP](https://github.com/peterall/junologue-chorus/blob/88bd17537afbe305e1b7d6476732ff09a9eae46d/junologue-chorus.cpp#L119-L167) · [Alpha Juno rate measurements](https://eokuwwy.blogspot.com/2021/06/roland-alpha-juno-12mks-50-chorus-rates.html)

The digital model shares one input history between its two taps and filters each return independently. At the FM-1 sample rate the nominal 3.5 ms center and ±34% clock sweep put the delay at roughly 2.6–5.3 ms. Those endpoints are voicing choices. The wet filters approximate the service circuit's response. The dry oscillator attack stays broadband. Modeling stops at the signal returned by the delays. The chip's individual charge transfers, analog noise and detector-controlled expansion are outside the model. [Panasonic MN3009 data sheet](https://www.ka-electronics.com/images/pdf/Panasonic_BBD.pdf)
